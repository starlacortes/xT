\section{Articles of Organization (Ohio Form 533A)}

File online at \textbf{Ohio Business Central} (\url{https://business.ohio.gov})
--- same-day processing. The paper Form 533A is only needed if you mail it; the
online form asks the same questions.

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}L{5.0cm}L{11.0cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Form field} & \textbf{Entry} \\
\midrule
Article 1 --- Name & \code{Isocline Labs LLC} \\
Article 2 --- Effective date & Leave blank (effective on filing date) \\
Article 3 --- Period of existence & Leave blank (defaults to perpetual) \\
Article 4 --- Purpose & \code{Any lawful purpose} \\
Article 5 --- Statutory agent & Your full legal name, plus your Ohio street
address (a P.O. box is \textbf{not} acceptable; must be a physical street
address in Ohio). \\
Article 6 --- Additional provisions & Leave blank (add indemnification language
only if your attorney advises) \\
Signatory & Your name and signature as authorized representative \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{Statutory agent notes}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.1em]
\item The agent must maintain a physical presence at the listed Ohio address
      during business hours to accept service of process.
\item \textbf{Your name and address become public record} on the Ohio SOS
      business search. If you do not want your home address public, use a
      commercial registered-agent service instead.
\item Ohio does not require a separate ``agent acceptance'' form when the agent
      is the same person signing the Articles.
\end{itemize}

\section{Operating Agreement}

Ohio law requires an operating agreement (not filed with the state, but you
must keep one with your records). Sign it yourself --- as single-member you sign
both as ``Member'' and as ``Manager.''

\noindent
Why it matters even though you are the only owner: it is the document that
proves the LLC is a real, separate entity and not your personal alter ego. That
separation is what keeps personal assets protected and is what a bank or
investor will ask to see.

\section{EIN (IRS Form SS-4) --- free, online}

Apply at \url{https://www.irs.gov/ein} (the online assistant issues the number
instantly during business hours).

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{@{}L{5.0cm}L{11.0cm}@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{SS-4 field} & \textbf{Entry} \\
\midrule
Legal name & \code{Isocline Labs LLC} \\
Entity type & Limited liability company \\
Number of members & 1 (``disregarded entity'' for federal tax --- profits/losses
flow to your personal return) \\
Reason for applying & ``Started a new business'' \\
Responsible party & Your name + SSN (the IRS requires a natural person, not the LLC) \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\noindent
A single-member LLC with no employees does not strictly need an EIN --- you can
use your SSN --- but an EIN is free, keeps your SSN off vendor/bank forms, and
most banks require one to open a business account. Get it.

\section{Post-formation checklist (in order)}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.2em]
\item[$\square$] File Articles of Organization (\$99) --- save the
      confirmation/certificate.
\item[$\square$] Sign and keep the operating agreement.
\item[$\square$] Apply for EIN (free).
\item[$\square$] Open a \textbf{business bank account} in the LLC's name (bring
      Articles + EIN + operating agreement). Do not mix personal and business
      funds from day one.
\item[$\square$] Register for Ohio \textbf{Commercial Activity Tax (CAT)} only
      if/when taxable gross receipts exceed \$150{,}000/year --- not relevant at
      launch. No other Ohio LLC tax registration is automatic.
\item[$\square$] Update the trademark worksheet: the ``Owner of Mark'' is now
      \code{Isocline Labs LLC}, organized in Ohio. This unblocks the TEAS Plus
      filing.
\item[$\square$] File the trademark (TEAS Plus, Class~9, \S1(b)) once the LLC
      is formed and the class~9 clearance search is done.
\item[$\square$] Enroll in the \textbf{App Store Small Business Program} and
      align it with the LLC + developer account.
\end{itemize}

\section{What not to worry about yet}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=0.1em]
\item \textbf{No Ohio annual report fee} --- Ohio LLCs pay no annual report;
      nothing is due every year at the state level.
\item \textbf{No publication requirement} --- Ohio does not require newspaper
      publication (unlike NY/NE).
\item \textbf{No registered-agent acceptance filing} when you are your own
      agent.
\end{itemize}
