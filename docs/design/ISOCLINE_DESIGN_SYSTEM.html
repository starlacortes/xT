01Principles

The sovereignty of the curve

The body is a continuous rhythm, not a checklist. The modeled curve is the hero of every surface that shows a level; streaks, badges and scores don't exist.

Calm empirical coordinates

Isocline presents measurements, ranges and rates of change — never verdicts. No "good/bad", no red/green status, no congratulation. The reader decides what a number means.

Discretion first

Nothing — icon, notification, lock-screen glance, printed page — may out a person as being on HRT. When brand presence and discretion conflict, discretion wins.

What is, and what you adjust · working principle

Two kinds of content, two kinds of surface. What simply is (who you are, where your level is now, what was measured) sits flush on the paper, with no card, border or container. It is reference, like the "I." of the mark: a statement, not a control. What you can adjust or act on (doses, settings, entries, the next thing to do) sits in a card. So the Home readout and the Settings profile are flush, and the dose card and the section cards are cards. When it's unclear which a piece of content is, ask whether tapping it would change something.

Still being developed. Treat it as a strong default, not yet a rule.

02The logo

The word is the logo, and its shape never changes. Like Apple's apple, ISOCLINE has exactly one outline. What changes is its rendering — fill, size, ground and surroundings. That is what lets it sit anywhere: a lock screen, a journal cover, a lab report, an embossed card.

The wordmark

  • Source: IBM Plex Serif SemiBold, all caps, tracking +0.07em, outlined to paths by tools/scripts/build_isocline_marks.py. It is never typed as live text — anyone can type "ISOCLINE" in Plex Serif; only the master outline is the logo.
  • Customization: intentionally light today (two optical kerning pairs). A type-designer pass to make distinctive letterform cuts — strengthening it as a trademark — is on the roadmap.
  • No curve. The earlier rise-and-plateau stroke under the word is retired. The curve lives in the product, not the logo.

The small mark — "I."

Where the word can't be read (app icon, favicon, notification badge, watch), the logo reduces to "I.": the wordmark's own I, followed by a perfect-circle point. The point is a plotted coordinate — a single measurement — and it reads, quietly, as the end of a sentence. Its diameter is the width of Plex Serif's own period, so it carries the weight the type gives punctuation; the gap is the font's native I→period spacing.

Three renderings, one shape

All renderings are emitted from identical path data — brand/isocline-{wordmark,mark}-{ink,hairline,emboss}.svg.

Ink & seed hue

The default. Solid ink on paper, or filled in the user's chosen hue. App splash, web header, Research title pages.

Engraved hairline

Outline only, at hairline weight, like a survey plate. Loading states, empty states, chart watermarks, large display uses.

Emboss / deboss

Inkless relief, after Omar Rayo's intaglios: paper on paper, legible only through light and shadow. The discretion rendering — the stealth icon, packaging, print covers.

Clear space & minimum size

  • Clear space: the cap height of the O on every side. Nothing enters it.
  • Wordmark minimum: 96px wide on screen, 22mm in print. Below that, use "I.".
  • "I." minimum: 16px. On icons it sits optically centred, never touching the mask.

Never

Re-type it

Live text, other weights, other fonts, other tracking.

Distort it

Stretch, skew, outline-in-another-weight, rotate, or set it on a curve.

Decorate it

Add the old curve, the ◉ bullseye, gradients inside the letters, glows, or drop shadows. Gradients belong to grounds, not to the mark.

04Color

Every color is a recipe, not a value. Each token is an OKLCH lightness, chroma and hue offset from the seed, except two that pin an absolute hue: day paper (cream) and the safety red. The seed defaults to the rose of #D4537E; the user may turn it anywhere. Out-of-gamut results lose chroma, never lightness; text is contrast-repaired against its ground to WCAG 4.5:1 (7:1 with Increase Contrast).

Paper — day
Night paper — dark

Paper and night paper

Day paper is cream at every dial position: an absolute OKLCH hue of 86.4° at L .965 / C .0155, which is #F8F3E8; the card is a lighter cream, #FDFAF3. The seed doesn't tint the paper; it lives in the ink's faint tint, the accent, the curve and its fill. Night paper takes its lightness and chroma from the Stitch dark reference (#151022, L .19 / C .036) and keeps the seed's hue, so at the default rose it lands on deep plum. Day and night follow the system: the app via iOS appearance, the web via the browser's native prefers-color-scheme and color-scheme: light dark.

Fixed colors

safety · #BA3630 / #F47C70Documented clinical thresholds only (see below). Never themed.
success · #3B8C6ELegacy: drew the old dashed trough lines. Retired from the chart (the range band is accent, §06); removed with the Home pass.
sage · #8FA382A secondary data series. Never a status color.

Values outside your range are shown by position against the band, never by color. One exception: a documented clinical threshold (E2 above 1,000 pg/mL; an E2 trough below 50, or below 80 with spironolactone; T above 1,500 ng/dL) takes the safety red as the outline, text and glyph of its pill. Never a fill, never on the curve, and never hidden behind calibration or a paywall. It states the clinical association; it doesn't instruct.

Rules

  • Every role scales with Dynamic Type: Font.custom(name, size:, relativeTo:) with the text style shown.
  • Mono labels never wrap mid-word. Give them lineLimit(1) + minimumScaleFactor(0.8), or shorten the copy. (The Stitch export broke "HOME" into "HOM/E" — the canonical example of what not to ship.)
  • Numbers are always mono, with tabular figures, and always carry their unit.

06Instrument vocabulary

The Home curve is the hero: a full-height, softly filled curve, from the Stitch Home study, drawn on paper. An isocline is a line of equal slope, the plateau where rate of change holds steady. The chart's job is to make that shape legible at a glance, and to be honest about where it comes from without making the user decode line styles.

Modeled level · seed-tinted fill · hollow ring = a lab draw · concentric point = now · band = your range

The fill

A vertical gradient under the curve, from levelTint at roughly 30% opacity down to transparent paper. It follows the hue dial like everything else. No glow on paper; a faint glow is allowed on night paper only.

The stroke

One continuous line, 2.5pt hero / 1.8pt elsewhere, sweeping levelLow → levelHigh along its length (the Stitch rose → lavender, now seed-relative). No dashed segment. Most people draw labs once or twice a year, so the curve is almost always modeled, and a permanent dash would mean nothing.

Provenance line

Under the readout, in mono: MODELED · CALIBRATED TO 2 LABS · LAST 4 MO AGO, or MODELED · NO LABS YET. This carries the measured-vs-modeled message the dash used to carry. The curve is always shown, with or without a lab; the provenance line is what says which.

Lab points

Each lab draw sits on the chart as a hollow ring at its measured value, the only measured marks. If the model and the draw disagree, the gap is visible and honest.

Now

A concentric point (a 15% halo around a solid dot) on the curve at the current time. It is the same perfect-circle point as the "I." mark.

Range & phases

The user's own range is a soft band labeled YOUR RANGE 100–600, spanning the trough floor to the peak ceiling they set, with gridline values at the edge. With no range set there is no band: clinical defaults aren't "yours". Phase words (PEAK, STEADY, TROUGH, DOSE) name pharmacokinetic position, never goals. No "target", no "limit".

Hairline engraving stays the rule everywhere else: ink at 14% for rules, 5% for grid. Gauges elsewhere (e.g. the Time tab) settle like an analog needle, with one damped swing and no spinner. The double rule and value-along-the-line devices from the October 8 reference plate are retired in favor of the readout and provenance line.

07Surfaces

Flush

No surface at all: type and imagery straight on the paper. Reserved for what is (readouts, the profile, measured values). See the working principle in §01.

Paper card

Card color, a 1px ink hairline at 14%, radius 16–20. No shadow. Depth is read from the line.

Emboss surface

For discreet states: content as relief on paper, no ink. Same treatment as the emboss logo.

Glass — system chrome only

Liquid Glass appears only where iOS 26 supplies it: the floating tab bar and sheets. Never layer glassEffect on content — inside a sheet, the sheet's own glass is the card surface.

Radii: 4 · 6 · 8 · 10 · 12 · 16 · 20 · 24. Spacing: 2 · 4 · 6 · 8 · 10 · 12 · 14 (card) · 16 · 20 · 24 · 28 · 32; screen gutter 22. Both mirror Theme.Radius / Theme.Spacing.

Home

The curve fills the screen behind everything, as it does in the app today. The readout and provenance line sit flush at the top, the phase strip sits under the curve, and one overlay card is docked above the tab bar. It holds the dose section and the latest journal entry. The injection site isn't on Home: the record-dose sheet shows the last site and suggests the next one, at the moment it matters.

The dose section: two states

The card changes height with the day instead of keeping a fixed height and leaving a gap.

  • Dose day: "Today". Serif time, countdown chip, mono medication line, and the filled Log Dose pill. This is the card at full height.
  • Blank day: "Next dose". One line: a mono NEXT DOSE label, the serif day and time, and the countdown chip pushed to the right. No filled pill, so the card gets one row shorter and the curve gets the space back. Tapping the row still opens the record-dose sheet, for early or off-schedule logging.
  • The card's height animates with the state change (a short spring, honoring Reduce Motion), and the journal section moves up with it.

Journal

Week strip, then the entries. Only the top entry is a card. It's the one still open to you: recent, editable, and the natural next thing to touch. Everything older is flush: rows straight on the paper, separated by ink hairlines that carry the hierarchy, and receding into a quiet record. This is the "what is / what you adjust" principle applied in time: the past simply is.

That hierarchy is the encouragement. The one card on the page is always "now", so a day with no entry yet shows a ghost card in its place: the outline of a card, drawn as a single ink hairline with no fill, like the engraved-hairline rendering of the logo. It is not a control and not a surface, just the shape of something not yet written, with one line of text: Tap "New entry" below to write in your journal. It points to the one action instead of duplicating it. New entry stays as the floating action pill above the tab bar, resting on a short paper fade so rows scroll beneath it. No streaks, counts or nudges: the invitation is spatial, not a notification.

Settings

The profile sits flush on the paper: avatar, serif name and a mono line, with no card, because it is who you are and not a control. Below it are the section cards: everything adjustable. Every icon tile uses the one seed accent, so the page reads as a calm column, and the subtitles carry the live values.

Profile avatar

Settings opens on the user's profile, flush on the paper: avatar, name, and a mono line beneath. Editing happens on tap, in a sheet; the profile itself stays reference. The avatar is a circle with a 1px ink hairline ring (no gradient ring, no glow). It shows the user's own photo, chosen from their library or camera and stored only on-device / in their iCloud. Without a photo it falls back to the seed-tinted placeholder: an accent disc with a person.fill glyph in on-accent ink, which follows the hue dial. Today that's ProfileManager.profilePhoto / renderPlaceholder and SettingsView+Header.swift. The avatar lives inside the app only: never on the icon, in notifications, or in widgets. Design mockups use the placeholder, never stock photography.

Appearance: the hue specimen

The hue picker opens on a specimen: a near-square window (radius specimen, continuous corners, 1 : 1.08) of the app in miniature, drawn with sample data so it never shows a person's real numbers. It may be screenshotted, and it works before setup. It holds one of each thing the hue drives:

  • the readout and provenance line;
  • the filled curve with its sweep, lab ring, now point and range band;
  • the phase strip;
  • a dose card with Log Dose;
  • chips, an icon tile, a toggle and the app icon.

Below the specimen:

  • a Day/Night preview, which applies to the specimen only;
  • the hue band;
  • eight quick stops, the same stops as the alternate icons;
  • a mono readout with Default rose.

Rules

  • Sample data, always. The specimen never reads the person's doses, labs or name.
  • Real components, real sizes. Build it from paperCard, Pill, ActionPill and the curve shapes, not a scaled screenshot. Inside the specimen only, Dynamic Type is capped at Large, because it's a preview.
  • One control. To VoiceOver the specimen is a single image with a summary label. The band is the control: adjustable, ±10° per swipe.
  • Night preview applies the night palette to the specimen alone (a colorScheme override). The screen around it follows the system.
  • Quick stops come from seed.stops in the tokens. The alternate icons use the same list, so a stop and its icon always match.
  • The same picker appears in onboarding, where the specimen shows someone the app before they've entered anything.

Dose notification layout

People choose what a dose reminder says on their lock screen. There are four layouts, and the default is Discreet, which names nothing. Custom lets someone pick each field and even write their own words.

The preview is built by the same content builder the app uses to schedule, fed sample data, so what you see is what arrives. iOS draws the banner itself:

  • the app's name and icon always show, so pair Discreet with the Discreet icon;
  • when the phone hides previews while locked, only the placeholder shows.

Previews hidden while locked

Rules

  • Default: Discreet. "Reminder" / "Your scheduled entry is ready." It names no medication, dose or body part.
  • One home. Settings → Medication & Dose → Reminders, next to the reminder times.
  • Fields: medication, amount, route, scheduled time, suggested site. Title: Reminder, Dose reminder, Isocline, or their own words (up to 40 characters, stored on this device only, never synced).
  • The action reads Open or Log dose. Both open the record-dose sheet; nothing is logged from the lock screen.
  • Hidden previews always show the placeholder "Reminder".
  • Journal reminders use the same title, and their body never carries health data.
  • The banner is iOS's own glass. This is a mock, not a component we draw.

09Navigation

Four destinations, in this order: Home · Journal · Time · Settings — already the order in Elements/Home/MainTabView.swift. Use the native iOS 26 tab bar (its floating glass pill comes free); tint the active item with the accent. No custom pill bar. One icon family (SF Symbols, regular weight) and one label style (mono caps) across every tab.

10Voice

Write
  • "218 pg/mL · inside your range"
  • "YOUR RANGE 100–600" · "PEAK" · "TROUGH"
  • "Modeled from your last lab, 14 days ago"
  • "Lower than this time last cycle"
  • "No entry yet today"
Not
  • "Great job! Levels look good 🎉"
  • "Warning: level too low"
  • "You missed a dose!"
  • "7-day streak"
  • "TARGET PEAK" · "TROUGH LIMIT"

Copy describes position, direction and provenance — never value judgment. docs/app-design/TIME_REGION_COPY.md has range/"warning" phrasing to revisit against this standard.

12Lineage & influences

Design rationale · internal and Research "about" only — never app UI or marketing

American survey & space-age

The USGS topographic quadrangle is the model for the hairline: contour lines are isolines, engraved at a weight that recedes until you need it. Apollo-era mission control is the model for the readout — calm telemetry in mono, numbers that carry their units, nothing that flashes.

Omar Rayo (Colombia)

Rayo's intaglios — prints pressed into paper with no ink at all, the form existing only as relief and shadow — are the source of the emboss rendering. They prove a mark can be wholly present and nearly invisible at once, which is exactly what discretion asks of this brand.

A quiet trans lineage

Isocline is built by and for trans people, and its materials carry three debts, acknowledged here rather than advertised:

  • Lynn Conway, who co-founded the VLSI revolution in chip design, was fired by IBM in 1968 for transitioning; IBM apologized publicly in 2020. Isocline's type system is IBM Plex. Setting a trans-built instrument in IBM's own typeface is a small, deliberate act of reclamation.
  • The synthesizer envelope — attack, decay, sustain, drawn on an oscilloscope — is the rise-and-plateau shape of a dosing curve. The trans composer who brought the Moog synthesizer to a mass audience in 1968 is honored here through that shape rather than by name — she has long asked that her work, not her transition, be the story.
  • Cassils' Cuts: A Traditional Sculpture recorded a body changing over 23 weeks, photographed daily — the body as a measured time series, which is what Isocline makes private and gentle.

Verify dates and specifics against primary sources before any of this appears on a public Research page.

13Licensing

  • IBM Plex (Serif, Mono) — SIL Open Font License 1.1, reserved font name "Plex". Free for commercial use, app embedding, and logos. Bundle the license; don't sell the font files alone; don't ship a modified font under the Plex name. Text: brand/licenses/IBM-Plex-OFL.txt.
  • Atkinson Hyperlegible Next — SIL Open Font License 1.1 in the Google Fonts release (googlefonts/atkinson-hyperlegible-next), with no reserved font name. Free for commercial use and app embedding; subsetting is allowed. Text: brand/licenses/Atkinson-Hyperlegible-Next-OFL.txt. Bundle that release. The Braille Institute's own downloads, and the copies TeX Live ships, carry the Braille Institute's license instead, which forbids derivatives; the print template may keep using TeX Live's copies, unmodified.
  • In the app: both notices go in an Acknowledgements screen (roadmap phase 1).
  • The logo: outlining and customizing the wordmark makes it our artwork; the font licenses don't restrict that. A plain-typed wordmark would be weak as a trademark — hence the master outline and the planned letterform pass.
  • Trademark: run a clearance search on "Isocline" (software/health classes) with counsel before significant spend.
