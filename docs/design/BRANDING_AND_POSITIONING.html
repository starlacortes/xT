Isocline

Most trackers count doses. Isocline shows you the curve.

1 · What the word means — and why it fits

An isocline is the curve along which solutions of a differential equation share the same slope — the phase-plane technique for visualizing how a system behaves over time. In biology, the zero-growth isocline is the boundary where a system's rate of change is zero: equilibrium, the steady state.

For this product the fit is close to exact:

  • The moat is literally differential equations. Ester-specific Bateman curves fit against published PK literature (Bi 2018, Ichihara 2020, Pastuszak 2021). An isocline is the tool for sketching ODE solutions — the name quietly encodes the rigor that separates xT from log books with reminders bolted on.
  • "Iso" = equal, steady, stable. A hormone protocol is pursuing an equilibrium. Users never need the math for the name to feel right; scientifically literate users, clinicians, and grant reviewers get a bonus layer of credibility.
  • Gender-neutral and clinical-adjacent, not clinical-cold. Works equally for trans masc and trans femme protocols — nothing about "Isocline" codes either way, unlike any name built on hormone shorthand.

Cautions. The word is opaque to most people — but so were Stripe, Notion, and Linear at launch. Make the math meaning the lore, never the pitch: it belongs on a "Why Isocline?" page and in pitch decks, not in App Store copy. Settle pronunciation once (EYE-so-cline) and use it consistently in any video or audio.

2 · Collision scan — trademark & discoverability

The only software product found using the name is daanx/isocline, an open-source GNU readline alternative — a developer CLI library in a different market entirely. Everything else in the search results is math and ecology education content (Desmos, Wolfram, MIT OCW).

That is about as clean as a real English word gets, but before spending money:

  • Run a USPTO knockout search, Classes 9 and 42 (software / SaaS).
  • Search the App Store for "isocline" before committing to paid trademark work.
  • Check domain and handle availability: isocline.app, isocline.health, @isocline on the channels that matter. Low collision risk so far suggests good odds.

SEO reality check. Math education pages own the organic results for the bare word. That is good for brand searches — once the app exists, "isocline app" and "isocline hrt" resolve instantly to you — but it means the naked term can never be an acquisition channel. Nobody searches it with intent, so nothing is lost.

The "Why Isocline?" lore page

One page, one graphic, three sentences: a phase-plane slope field with a single highlighted isocline, annotated "your hormone journey is a curve, not a checklist." That single image does the naming, the differentiation, and the trust story at once — and none of the competitors could publish it honestly. Keep it off the App Store listing; it is a landing-page and pitch-deck asset.

Voice

Keep the register the business plan already calls for: calm, precise, non-clinical-cold, not twee. The name tilts slightly clinical, so the design compensates — the soft rose/lavender palette and plain-language copy carry the warmth. The name brings the rigor; the design brings the care.

4 · Theming & color

UPDATED OCT 10, 2026 — The lowercase monoline wordmark, the curve-as-letterform, and the named curve-phase themes below are superseded by §7. The hue dial stays free (no named themes); the visual system is Paper & Instrument. Canonical spec: ISOCLINE_DESIGN_SYSTEM.html.

The existing assets are strong: an OKLCH hue-rotation theme engine, glass cards, and a soft rose/lavender default. Recommendation: build the brand identity on them rather than starting over.

  • Keep rose/lavender as the default theme. Every competitor and every clinic portal defaults to blue or teal; that is precisely the association to avoid. The lavender default differentiates from sterile medical apps and from the dark-steel, masc-coded TRT trackers, and reads as "made with care" without being loud about it.
  • Name the themes after curve phases. (Superseded — the dial stays free.) The user-selectable hue dial becomes brand storytelling: Rising (warm rose/coral), Peak (violet), Trough (deep indigo), Steady (muted sage or the default). The theme picker turns into an onboarding moment that teaches the product's mental model.
  • Adopt the curve as the core visual motif. (Superseded for the logo — the curve lives in the product, not the wordmark.) A lowercase isocline wordmark in a geometric monoline sans, where one letterform — the l or the second i — is drawn as a rise-and-plateau Bateman curve, with a point marker on it. The icon version is the curve mark alone on a soft gradient; it reads at small sizes and is ownable.
  • Make honest data the visual signature. The app already shows uncalibrated instead of faking precision — formalize that as a brand device: dashed vs. solid curve states, a distinct "modeled" texture. In every screenshot and ad, the honesty is the brand.
  • Type pairing. A warm humanist sans for prose; a monospace or technical face for dosages, units, and values — the instrument-panel feel that says measurement, not diary. Tabular numerals everywhere digits align.
  • Accessibility as a claim. The OKLCH engine's contrast repair already guarantees WCAG through the hue dial — say so publicly ("designed colorimetrically"). It is true and nobody else in the space can say it.

5 · Ad style & creative direction

Channels are already validated: Reddit, Discord, TransVitae — where competitor apps are getting discovered. The creative should look native to a data-literate community:

  • Data-viz forward, not people forward. Ads built from real app screens — actual curves, actual honest uncalibrated labels. No stock-photo couples, and crucially no before/after body imagery: it is the space's ugly failure mode, it is community-hostile, and it sails into health-ad-policy trouble on Meta and Google. Graphs sail through review and convert the user the product actually wants.
  • Founder-led, not banner-led. The highest-credibility placement is a post, not an ad: "I built the tracker I couldn't find — it models ester pharmacokinetics, here's the math," with a curve screenshot. A solo trans founder publishing their own PK research memos is a story no VC-backed competitor can copy.
  • Tone check per ad: calm, precise, zero exclamation points, no urgency or FOMO. The name sets a quiet register; the design carries the emotion.

6 · Verdict

Isocline is a genuinely strong pick: ownable, collision-light, gender-neutral, and secretly descriptive of the moat. Ship it with rose/lavender as the default, the curve as the identity, "the tracker that shows you the curve" as the position, and real data visualization leading every piece of creative.

The Apple model

The word is the logo, and its shape never changes. Only its rendering does — fill, size, ground, surroundings — which is what lets it sit anywhere.

Wordmark

All-caps ISOCLINE from IBM Plex Serif SemiBold, tracking +0.07em, outlined to paths (masters in docs/design/brand/, built by tools/scripts/build_isocline_marks.py). Never typed as live text. No curve beneath it. A type-designer pass on the letterforms is planned to strengthen it as a trademark.

Small mark — "I."

Where the word can't be read, the logo reduces to "I." — the wordmark's own I and a perfect-circle point, a single plotted coordinate. App icon, favicon, badges.

Renderings

  • Ink & seed hue — the default.
  • Engraved hairline — outline only, like a survey plate.
  • Emboss / deboss — inkless relief after Omar Rayo's intaglios; the discretion rendering and the stealth icon.

Architecture

One logo, three arms, distinguished by a mono descriptor: ISOCLINE (the app), ISOCLINE / RESEARCH, ISOCLINE / LABS. The app icon is "I." on a soft seed-hue ground that follows the user's dial; the stealth icon is "I." embossed on plain paper.

Visual system

Paper & Instrument: cream paper by day, seed-tinted night paper by dark (following the system appearance), hairline engraving, mono readouts, and a provenance line instead of dashes for modeled data. Lineage: American survey and space-age instrumentation, and Omar Rayo's relief prints. Type: IBM Plex Serif (display), IBM Plex Mono (values), Atkinson Hyperlegible Next (prose).

Brand principles

  • Discretion. Nothing may out a user as being on HRT at a glance — discretion beats brand presence by default. The name itself is the first discretion asset.
  • Observations, not verdicts. No good/bad, no red/green status, no streaks.
