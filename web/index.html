A real pharmacokinetic model

Ester-specific absorption and elimination curves fit against published clinical literature — not a straight line between injections. Cypionate, enanthate, undecanoate, and transdermal routes each behave the way the research says they do.

Injection sites that actually rotate

An anatomical body map that remembers every site, shows you how long each one has been resting, and steers you toward the ones that are ready. Scar tissue is a real problem; guessing is not a plan.

Calibrated to your bloodwork

Enter a lab result and the model adjusts to you — because the published averages were never going to be your numbers. Labs and doses live on one timeline, so you can see what your protocol change actually did.

Why I'm building this

I built byTime because I needed it and the thing I needed didn't exist. I wanted to know what my levels were doing between blood draws, whether the dip at day six was real or in my head, and whether I was quietly wrecking the same two injection sites over and over.

So I read the pharmacokinetics literature and built the model properly. Where the research is solid, byTime is precise. Where it isn't — propionate, for one — the app says so instead of inventing a number. That's the whole design principle: you deserve an honest instrument, not a confident-sounding one.

On your data

Your hormone levels, your labs, and your journal are some of the most sensitive data you have, and trans people have every reason to be careful about where it lives. byTime is built to keep your data on your device. It is never sold, never used for advertising, and never shared with anyone.

A full plain-language privacy policy will be published here before the beta opens — not buried in a wall of legalese after you've already signed up.

byTime is not a medical device. It estimates hormone levels from a pharmacokinetic model. It does not diagnose, does not replace bloodwork, and does not tell you how to dose. Talk to your prescriber.
